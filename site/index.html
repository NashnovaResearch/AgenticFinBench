How to read these results

“Hallucinated responses” is the share of 500 answers containing at least one claim that is both ungrounded in the agent's trace and externally refuted. It is not a complete factual-error rate. An unverified claim is not automatically a hallucination, and trace support does not establish factual correctness.

Values are transcribed from the current manuscript tables, rounded as reported. Differences may not equal the subtraction of displayed rounded values. The source manifest records figure and table hashes. The earlier 12-configuration snapshot in results.json is preserved separately and is not used for this leaderboard. The standalone runner's legacy diagnostics are described in the repository README.

03 / FROM REAL REQUESTS TO A BENCHMARK

Data Construction Process

Real requests. Careful selection. Preserved intent.

Questions originate in production logs of a financial assistant. Three selection batches are filtered, sampled, anonymized, and reviewed to produce a frozen evaluation set.

01

Collect & screen

14,697 query records and 23,731 production traces provide candidates. Filters remove duplicates, unsuitable or out-of-scope queries, and requests dependent on missing context.

02

Sample & anonymize

Seed, expansion, and English batches contribute 83, 297, and 120 final questions. Experts remove personal information while retaining the original intent and phrasing.

03

Review & freeze

A second expert review removes unclear questions. Frozen question-specific rubrics contain 12,786 positive criteria and 3,856 negative criteria.

The benchmark covers selected real requests; it is not a probability sample of production traffic. Batch composition (380 + 120) differs from language composition (374 Chinese + 126 English).

04 / EVIDENCE-INFORMED EVALUATION

From a response to a defensible score.

Question-specific rubrics assess the work. A two-stage claim audit checks evidence attribution and factual reliability. Audit evidence then informs the final quality score.

01 / RUBRICS

Did it answer the request?

Positive and negative criteria assess task completion and analytical quality without requiring a single reference answer.

02 / CLAIM AUDIT

What supports the facts?

Claims are checked against the execution trace; ungrounded claims are then checked with external evidence.

03 / QUALITY

How much does an error matter?

Audit evidence affects the quality score according to the scope, severity, and downstream impact of the error.

05 / WHAT THE EXPERIMENTS REVEAL

Key Findings

The answer changes when you check the facts.

01

Factual auditing changes the ranking.

Pi leads under the rubric-only baseline, but loses 6.27 points after the audit and moves to second. GPT-5.6 Sol loses 0.28 points and moves from fourth to first.

−6.27points for Pi
after factual auditing

Point-estimate ranks; adjacent configurations may not differ significantly.

02

Small claim-level error rates reach many answers.

Claim-level hallucination rates range from 0.03% to 2.46%, yet 0.8%–43.8% of responses contain at least one hallucinated claim. Density and reach tell different stories.

0.8–43.8%responses containing
a hallucinated claim

Hallucination here means ungrounded and externally refuted. It is not a complete factual-error rate.

03

The harness can matter more than the model.

Holding DeepSeek V4.1 Flash fixed, quality spans 11.68 points across Pi, DSH, and WorkBuddy. Holding Codex CLI fixed, the four GPT models span 8.48 points.

11.68point quality spread
across three harnesses

These contrasts are observational: instructions, tools, and reasoning settings also vary.

04

Financial data is associated with stronger grounding.

Across nine configurations with complete traces, financial-data use is associated with 16.6 percentage points more grounded claims after controlling for configuration and category. This is an association, not a causal estimate.

05

Complex tasks expose reliability gaps.

Averaged over configurations, hallucinated responses rise from 14.0% at L1 to 22.1% at L4. Longer, more complex answers expose more claims to error even when rubric scores remain high.
